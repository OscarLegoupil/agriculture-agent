# What the public frontier executes

The strongest reviewed references do not repeatedly solve the same unconstrained task-ranking problem as startup10. They execute coherent whole-season worker schedules, then apply limited observation-dependent market and recovery corrections. The useful architectural lesson is to make an investment plan carry an executable service calendar. Copying their farm counts or increasing our hiring cap does not reproduce their timing or production.

This is a source and saved-replay diagnosis, not a new benchmark. It examines frozen startup10 `febe9c76051a11e9ea7700e2d4701722e98274c51c50874ad03e1088b9398d4b`, and the pinned COK/Seyam artifacts in the challenge manifest. No reference code or schedules were copied into the policy. The broader development field has exposed the limited coverage of the original favorable two-seed screen; those seeds must not drive a new strength claim.

## Actual active architectures

**COK:** `_select_route` chooses from complete worker/action schedules such as 10 cows/4 sheep in three quadrants, 8/6 in three quadrants, or a larger sheep route following an early yarn store. Early public shop order is the primary branch signal. Observable rival layout can adjust a branch or trigger a guarded legacy opening. The step-72 fallback gate uses a specific public opening signature and relative cash; step 168 locks a compatible legacy shop branch. These are public-state decisions, not access to opponent identity or future shops.

The execution wrapper repairs weeds, aligns scheduled animal placements, reconciles scheduled cow purchases after a cash shortfall, clips surplus seed orders, and liquidates terminal goods. Its market layers can advance a planned sale and subtract the advanced quantity from a later scheduled sale. Knowing its own future worker schedule makes those inventory obligations concrete. A seemingly adaptive ninth-cow hook is **disabled** in this pinned artifact; it must not be cited as an active capability.

**Seyam:** the active `use_fixed_schedule=True`, version-v18 branch selects an embedded expert worker route. The loaded runtime has `board_by_seat` equal to `mohit` for both seats and `board_distance_strength=0.0`. Therefore the advertised observation-based board-fork mechanism is inactive in this artifact. Physical production follows that one route; once per day a feature-distance rule chooses a complete market expert with a stay bonus. It also applies order-slot recovery, same-turn shed projection, and terminal liquidation. The extensive generic `_build_tasks`/`_assign_actions` implementation is a fallback, not the normal source of these measured performances.

Both artifacts have public replay-derived ancestry. They are separate executable implementations and useful hard opponents, but they do not represent two wholly independent planning paradigms. The anchored crop and mixed agents still matter for coverage. Embedded routes and model parameters are legal inputs under the reviewed observation-only implementation; this review does not independently certify competition rules or live ratings.

## The opening is now comparable; the second cohort differs

Saved seat-0 field replays for seeds 2002 and 2003 show that the startup correction has largely closed the first-day allocation gap. Against COK, both farms have 12 melons, seven wheat, two cows, and two sheep at the first reset. The differences arise during the second investment wave, not from failing to imitate that initial footprint.

| Milestone, seed 2003 against COK | Startup10 | COK |
|---|---|---|
| Day 6, cows/sheep | 2 / 2 | 4 / 2 |
| Day 6, land quadrants | 2 | 1 |
| Day 9, cows/sheep | 4 / 3 | 6 / 4 |
| Day 12, cows/sheep | 4 / 4 | 8 / 6 |
| Day 12, strawberries/wheat | 33 / 7 | 37 / 17 |
| Day 16, strawberries/wheat | 43 / 7 | 38 / 12 |
| Day 20, strawberries/wheat | 43 / 7 | 38 / 23 |

The startup policy spends on expansion earlier, retains its two-cow/two-sheep cap until day 8, then often declines further animals using its economic screen. It commits to a larger strawberry cohort while COK maintains more animal co-production and ongoing wheat rotations. Against Seyam, the same qualitative shift appears: at day 9 in seed 2002, startup10 has five cows/two sheep and 22 strawberries; Seyam has five cows/six sheep and 12 strawberries. By day 16 their strawberry counts are similar, 42 versus 40.

The table samples actual tile states, not requested purchases. Hand counts at daily reset are zero for both farms because workers reset; they are not evidence of absent labor. Inspecting requested daily hires shows COK varying four to twelve hands across mature days, while startup10 spends long stretches at ten. That supports calendar-dependent service capacity rather than interpreting one fixed hiring cap as optimal.

## A missing co-product matters more than another threshold

The current animal admission equation values milk/wool/eggs, then subtracts purchase, daily wheat, and daily worker costs. It **does not credit fertilizer**. Fertilizer replenishes daily independently of feeding; collection, delivery, storage, sale timing, and any deliberate terminal abandonment still have to be paid for. Ignoring this revenue can reject precisely the additional animals that a feasible service plan can profitably support.

Official worker and market transitions were reconstructed for both players in two complete saved COK replays, matching all 2,876 player-cash checks. These are realized quantities and receipts, not the references' often oversized requested orders:

| Quantity / cash, seed 2003 | Startup10 | COK |
|---|---:|---:|
| Milk sold / receipts | 110 / 3,924 | 222 / 5,542 |
| Wool sold / receipts | 107 / 11,903 | 162 / 17,461 |
| Fertilizer sold / receipts | 153 / 9,619 | 228 / 14,742 |
| Fertilizer bought / expense | 35 / 1,696 | 0 / 0 |
| Wheat sold / receipts | 102 / 3,905 | 517 / 22,086 |
| Wheat bought / expense | 122 / 4,653 | 486 / 19,883 |
| Strawberries sold / receipts | 278 / 34,218 | 267 / 29,105 |

COK's fertilizer net receipts exceed startup10 by 6,819, wool by 5,558, and milk by 1,618. Startup10 offsets much of that with crop income, and loses this game by 3,220. These are accounting differences, **not additive causal gains from adding animals**: changing supply changes everyone's prices, capital, labor, and crop opportunities.

The wheat figures also reject a misleading inference. COK's gross wheat income is large, but it simultaneously buys 486 wheat. Its sale-minus-purchase cash is only 2,203 before seeds and labor, versus startup10's −748. Gross commercial-wheat sales cannot establish a 22,000 advantage. Seed 2002 tells a similar story: COK fertilizer net receipts are 12,990 versus 8,397, while startup10's strawberry receipts exceed COK by 11,864. Startup10 narrowly wins that replay, 110,823 to 110,301.

## Concrete architecture hypothesis

The next candidate should plan **small production-and-service bundles**, not a larger fixed farm or a copied action tape. A bundle contains a purchase/planting date, a small local set of tiles, assigned input supply, service dates, earliest deliveries, and terminal receipts. Value all its co-products and incremental worker demand together. Existing crops, inventory, and animals remain commitments with observed ages and sunk costs.

Start with the smallest discriminating implementation: at the day-3-to-day-8 capital decision, enumerate keeping cash, a local cow pair, a local sheep pair, and one additional strawberry cohort. Include the fertilizer generated before the last collect-and-sell opportunity; deduct actual feed obligations, collection actions, and extra travel. Score new animals against a no-addition supply scenario and a stressed own/opponent supply scenario. Reserve a daily service circuit before admitting the investment. Require cash runway through the first crop/animal receipts. This directly tests whether the current earlier land purchase and deferred animal cohort are the wrong use of working capital.

The scheduler should carry a short circuit to completion when its prerequisites remain valid: acquire feed once, service a nearby animal group, collect available product/fertilizer, and deliver only when economically useful or storage/deadlines require it. Rebuild a circuit when observed failure invalidates it or an urgent task becomes infeasible. This supplies the persistence that full reference routes obtain automatically, while retaining observation-based recovery. A one-turn assignment solver can remain the dispatcher, but it should choose among feasible multi-action circuits rather than independent destination values.

Initially evaluate circuits over a single official day offline and compare delivered units, missed feeds, pickup/delivery actions, and marginal labor against the incumbent on the same observed starting state. An approximate daily capacity calculation is acceptable for screening only after those probes show that it ranks feasible bundles correctly. Whole-season official replays must confirm any claimed win improvement; local replay counterfactuals must not freeze a reacting opponent and call that deployable strength.

This modest bundle enumeration has a specific purpose and a falsifiable bottleneck. A bespoke whole-game simulator, learned value model, or broad parameter search is not yet justified. If corrected co-product valuation plus executable local circuits cannot improve the second-cohort return, reject that hypothesis and measure where the remaining schedule value comes from before scaling the model.

## Market scope remains separate

COK often carries substantial premium inventory: seed 2002 day 25 has 65 wool in a full shed; seed 2003 day 29 has 35 wool in a full shed. Relative cash during the season therefore understates its liquidatable assets. Its 16,189 receipts in the final eleven actions of seed 2002 are a liquidation event, not sudden production. The separate market diagnosis found a cautious one-day reserve too weak to adopt. That negative result does not explain away the longer, joint storage decisions supported by a known production schedule.

The highest-value architectural change is consequently a plan that links cohort investment, animal co-products, local worker service, and inventory obligations. The public field's success does not establish that a general optimizer is intrinsically inferior to action tapes; it shows that isolated economically scored tasks have not yet delivered the same coherent plan.
