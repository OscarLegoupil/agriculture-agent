## Plateau diagnosis and discriminating experiments

The repeated four-seed panel appeared in 24–28 archived experiments. Treat it
as diagnostic fixtures, not reliable selection evidence. Larger 32-seed panels
rejected land-only, larger input batches and feeding variants. These reject the
implemented policies, not every method of routing or expanding the farm.
In two severe losses, shortening paths with task order fixed saves only 94/98
moves of 4,214/4,199. Task sequencing and production commitments remain largely
unchanged. Prior pickup-size experiments did not implement persistent circuits.

Three independently falsifiable directions:

1. **Coherent production calendar.** Explicit cumulative dairy/wool/balanced
   herd targets, earlier commissioning labor, sustained wheat, a bounded berry
   cohort and all four plots. Include initial production and manure in admission.
   This changes actual purchasing priorities rather than merely relaxing a cap.
   Measure placements, timing, receipts and labor; reject if added capacity cannot
   be serviced or its competitive improvement fails broader development.
2. **Multi-action local service.** Commit workers to feasible feed, care, harvest
   and manure circuits with shared input reservations and observed recovery.
   First test official daily transitions, then whole games. Distinguish fewer
   moves from additional delivered production and avoided missed feeding.
3. **Seasonal market dispatch.** Allocate already-owned inventory to legal sale
   opportunities under public demand/production scenarios and own price impact.
   Reserve storage and working capital. No future realized shops or rival private
   inventory. Validate the unit-price/market-clock assumptions in the interpreter.
